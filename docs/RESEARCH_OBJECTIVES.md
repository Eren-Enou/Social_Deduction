# Research objectives

Decided: validate a reliable environment for later causal social research. Experiment 001 does not test intentional deception, intelligence, calibrated belief, consciousness, or psychological realism.

Truth means correspondence with the engine's incident actor. Belief is an individual's candidate actor derived from its observation. A claim is a public assertion; communication intent is a separately recorded policy-selected category. False claims in this experiment may reflect mistaken observations and are not labeled lies.

Experiment 002 tests whether private incentives change selected communication under a transparent fixed scoring policy. Belief may also derive from a legitimate participation briefing. Intentional contradiction requires a known belief, contrary assertion, and a recorded contradictory strategy; sincere mistakes require belief-faithful assertions that are objectively false. Actual listener effects are evaluated separately from selection. This establishes narrow causal software behavior, not spontaneous intelligence or human lying.

Experiment 003 tests the causal chain from received testimony through local evidence scoring, belief change/retention, chosen ballot and resolved outcome. Original evidence and current support are distinct; scores are not probabilities. Listener inputs exclude observer truth and sender intentions. Selected communication and externally injected testimony are analyzed separately; behavioral influence can be incorrect or absent. Successful verdicts do not establish general reasoning ability.

Experiment 004 tests how legitimately visible source relationships alter support and judgment relative to counting speakers. Actual acquisition chains, perceived declared groups, proposition accuracy and chosen relay strategy are separate. Hidden dependence cannot be inferred from observer roots; matching references verify message relationships only. Faithful false relays need not be intentional contradictions. Controlled results demonstrate duplicate-support reductions, attribution-driven grouping errors and changed ballots, including worse verdicts; they do not demonstrate calibrated confidence, reliable witness independence, sophisticated skepticism or improved communication strategy.

Experiment 005 separates private belief change, voluntary public revision and recipient interpretation. Explicit same-speaker corrections/retractions alter current standing without rewriting the past. Local costs can suppress correction after a recognized mismatch. Visible branches/groups and missing references affect acceptance independently from objective accuracy; relays have authority over their own earlier statements only. Evaluations demonstrate bounded recovery, changed selected forwarding, uncertainty, wrong sincere/misleading revisions and failed propagation. Correct recovery means a previously wrong recipient becomes correct and acts on that belief; mere abstention or changed wrong answer is reported separately. No finding establishes remorse, calibrated trust, reliable corrections or sophisticated manipulation.
